# Series updated with the statistics of the batches signalled once to the host (2026-11-24)

Series `834940d61` = series `78fb1d845` + the design of the statistics of
the synchronous computations of the commit `2b13a11c6` (local branch
`statistics-locked`, 8 files: `Batch.hxx`, `GPU.hxx`, `SYCL.hxx`,
`Kokkos.hxx`, `RAJA.hxx` and `Arcane.hxx` of `MFront/GenericParallel`, and
the two pages of the `generic-parallel` interfaces). The `cuda`, `hip`,
`sycl`, `kokkos`, `raja` and `arcane` backends are concerned. The
generated code, the API of the libraries and the other backends are
unchanged.

In the series `78fb1d845`, every thread updating the statistics of a
block (every warp with CUDA and HIP) stored the flag of the block, in
pinned host memory, a block was reset by a synchronous copy after an
event, and the block was allocated with the flags of `hipHostAlloc`,
which HIP only defines since ROCm 6.3. In the new series, the first
thread recording an event, selected by an atomic operation on a flag of
the device, performs the only store of a call to the memory of the host,
the statistics are reset by the next call using the block, by a copy
performed before its computations, and the block is allocated by
`hipHostMalloc` (see the journal, entry of 2026-11-24).

## Cost of a synchronous call

Program `scratch/stats-locked/harness/stats.cxx`, `timing` mode:
synchronous evaluations of the material property
`UO2_YoungModulus_Martin1989`, without statistics given by the calling
code, on batches without event, with one event at the last point (at each
call, then at a call out of two) and with every point out of its bounds.
Each call is checked against its expected status and statistics.
Microseconds per call: median of the medians of the rounds (5 rounds of
at most 2000 calls on the RTX 5080, 3 to 5 rounds of at most 100 to 500
calls on the other devices, a round lasting at most about half a
second), best of the runs. With Kokkos, the batches with an event at
every point are limited to 1000 points (see below). Variants of the
runtime headers:

- copies: the statistics are reset before each call and copied back after
  it, as on the official master (design reproduced in the headers of the
  commit);
- series 3: headers of the series `78fb1d845`;
- series 4: headers of the commit `2b13a11c6`, in its own install of TFEL;
- series 4, shared install: the same headers installed from the series
  `834940d61`, measured during the validation below, the afternoon of the
  same day. The kernel of 2^20 points then ran about 4 µs faster for all
  the backends; the differences between the regimes are the same.

Sources, in `scratch/stats-locked/logs`: `final-timing.log` (copies and
series 4, two alternated runs), `timing-final.log` (series 3, one run),
`amd-igpu.log` and `extra.log` (AMD integrated GPU, without the
serialization of the kernels and of the copies), `cpu-devices.log` (SYCL

Reading of the tables:

- without event, the new series costs what the series 3 costs: the kernel
  and one synchronization, about 4 µs less than the copies with CUDA, HIP,
  Kokkos and RAJA, 8 µs less with Arcane and 13 to 14 µs less with SYCL,
  whatever the number of points;
- with one event per call, the series 3 cost about 4 µs more than the
  copies with CUDA, HIP, Kokkos and RAJA (synchronous reset of the block
  before its next use). The new series costs what the copies cost (3 µs
  less with SYCL): the reset is a copy performed before the computations,
  without additional synchronization. With an event at a call out of two,
  the cost is the mean of the two regimes;
- with an event at every point, the series 3 cost 622 to 627 µs at 2^20
  points with CUDA and HIP against 64 µs with the copies (one store to the
  memory of the host per warp), and 670 to 682 µs against 483 to 496 µs
  with SYCL, RAJA and Arcane (one store per point). The new series costs
  what the copies cost. The 483 to 496 µs of SYCL, RAJA and Arcane are
  the atomic operations of the statistics, performed point by point,
  whereas the kernels of CUDA and HIP first combine the events of a warp;
- on the AMD integrated GPU, the timings at 2^20 points vary from 1.6 to
  2.0 ms from one run to another for both variants;
- on the CPU device of DPC++, a call without event of 2^20 points takes
  327 µs against 274 µs with the copies and 302 µs with the series 3,
  although `signalEvents` is not executed in this regime: the difference
  comes from the code generated for the loop. Diagnostic variants of
  `signalEvents` (`logs/diag-icpx-1.log`): 272 µs with the store alone,
  279 µs with the read of the flag of the device and the store, 312 µs
  with the atomic operation and the store. Up to 1000 points, the call is
  faster than with the copies (9.2 against 13.5 µs).

Choices of the design measured with variants of the headers
(`logs/timing-exch.log`, `logs/timing-nopad.log`):

- flag of the device set by an addition rather than by an exchange with
  SYCL, RAJA and Arcane (the additions of the threads of a warp of an
  NVIDIA device are merged in a single atomic operation by its compiler,
  contrary to the exchanges): with an event at every point, 67.5 against
  77.9 µs at 10^5 points and 492 against 504 µs at 2^20 points with SYCL,
  57.4 against 68.0 µs and 483 against 493 µs with RAJA, 60.4 against
  64.5 µs and 486 against 495 µs with Arcane;
- flag of the device kept 128 bytes away from the statistics: with an
  event at every point, 15.6 against 16.3 µs at 10^5 points with CUDA, 482
  against 487 µs at 2^20 points with RAJA, 485 against 489 µs with Arcane.

Separate finding, not treated by the commit: on a CUDA device, Kokkos
performs the atomic operations on `unsigned long long` values, the type
of the statistics, by compare-and-swap loops: its native operations
(`desul/atomics/cuda`) are defined for `uint64_t`, which is another type
(`unsigned long`). One addition per point on a same counter
(`micro/kokkos-atomics.cpp`, `logs/kokkos-atomics.log`): 224 µs for 1000
points, 8.1 ms for 10^4, 0.44 s for 10^5. With an event at
every point, a call of the `kokkos` backend takes 250 µs at 1000 points
and 0.53 s at 10^5 points, with the copies as with the new series
(`logs/kokkos-extra.log`); 11.7 µs and 57 µs with a variant of the
backend performing them on `std::uint64_t` values (`logs/extra.log`).
With one event out of 128 points, a call on 10^5 points takes 171 µs
with the copies and 169 µs with the new series
(`logs/timing-kokkos-2.log`).
