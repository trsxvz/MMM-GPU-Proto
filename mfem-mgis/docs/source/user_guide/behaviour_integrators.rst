=====================
Behaviour integrators
=====================

A behaviour integrator computes residual and jacobian terms for a
material. Behaviour integrators are associated with:

- a physical phenomenon (mechanics, heat transfer, etc.),
- a modeling hypothesis (plane strain, plane stress, etc.),
- a material symmetry (isotropy, geometry).

.. contents::
    :depth: 3
    :local:

.. note::

   Behaviour integrators are generally declared using 
   the :code:`addBehaviourIntegrator` method of the
   :code:`NonLinearEvolutionProblem` class, as in the
   following example:

   .. code:: c++

      mechanics.addBehaviourIntegrator(ctx, "Mechanics", "beam",
                                       "src/libBehaviour.so",
                                       "MicromorphicDamageI_SpectralSplit");

   Based on the generic category :code:`Mechanics`, this declaration
   automatically handles the selection of
   a behaviour integrator consistent with the modeling hypothesis
   declared by the nonlinear evolution problem and the material
   symmetry declared by the behaviour.

   An optional last argument gives parameters to the behaviour
   integrator:

   - :code:`Regularization`: regularization of the :code:`Mechanics`
     behaviour integrators (:code:`FBar` or :code:`Faltus2026`, see
     below);
   - :code:`BehaviourExecutionLocation` (:code:`cpu` or :code:`gpu`): the
     location where the behaviour of this material is expected to be
     integrated, checked against its library (see
     :ref:`mfem_mgis_batch_computations_execution`);
   - :code:`DisplacementGradient` (boolean, :code:`false` by default):
     give the displacement gradient :math:`H = F - I` instead of the
     deformation gradient :math:`F` to a finite strain behaviour. The
     deformation gradient being close to the identity, its components are
     only known within about :math:`6 \cdot 10^{-8}` in single precision.
     The displacement gradient keeps the relative precision of small
     strains: the behaviours written in the Green-Lagrange or Hencky
     strain frameworks compute their strain from it without cancellation
     (the behaviours written with the deformation gradient receive
     :math:`F = I + H`). The gradients of the material, named
     :code:`DisplacementGradient`, are then the displacement gradients,
     and so are its macroscopic gradients (for instance the ones given by
     :code:`setMacroscopicGradientsEvolution` for a periodic problem): a
     caller which keeps the macroscopic displacement gradient keeps the
     precision of the macroscopic strain. The macroscopic gradients being
     the same for all the materials, the finite strain behaviours of a
     periodic problem shall all be given the deformation gradient, or all
     the displacement gradient. The post-processings of
     mfem-mgis use :math:`F = I + H`. This requires a behaviour generated
     by a version of :code:`MFront` which accepts it, and is not supported
     by the regularizations.

   All the behaviour integrators described below support the integration
   by batches and the computations by kernels, on the GPU if a GPU is used
   (except the regularization of Faltus et al. in plane stress, see
   :ref:`mfem_mgis_batch_computations`).

Behaviour integrators based on :code:`MFront` behaviours
========================================================

Most behaviour integrators rely on external behaviours that shall be
generated by :code:`MFront`. In practice, those external behaviours are
compiled by :code:`MFront` into shared libraries that are loaded
dynamically when a behaviour integrator is loaded.

Mechanical behaviour integrators
--------------------------------

A mechanical behaviour integrator computes the residual and jacobian
associated with one of the following variational operators:

.. math::
   
   \begin{aligned}
   \int_{\Omega_r} \underline{\sigma}\,\colon\,\underline{\varepsilon}^{\star}\,\mathrm{d}\,V & \quad\quad &\text{(small strain)} \\
   \int_{\Omega_r} \underline{P}\,\colon\,\underline{F}^{\star}\,\mathrm{d}\,V & \quad\quad & \text{(finite strain)}
   \end{aligned}

where:

- :math:`\Omega_r` is the domain occupied by the material of interest in the
  reference configuration,
- :math:`\underline{\sigma}` is the `Cauchy` stress (expressed in the
  reference configuration for a small strain analysis),
- :math:`\underline{\varepsilon}^{\star}` is the virtual deformation,
- :math:`\underline{P}` is the first Piola-Kirchhoff stress,
- :math:`\underline{F}^{\star}` is the virtual deformation gradient.

The mechanical behaviour integrators available in :code:`MFEM/MGIS`
support:

- small and finite strain behaviours,
- isotropic and orthotropic behaviours,
- plane strain, plane stress and tridimensional modeling hypotheses.

FBar formulations
^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^

The :math:`\bar{F}` method, or FBar formulation, is implemented following
:cite:`de_souza_neto_design_1996`. This formulation is designed to handle
nearly incompressible materials in large strain analysis by using a modified
deformation gradient :math:`\bar{\underline{F}}` that separates volumetric and
deviatoric responses.

The method replaces the standard deformation gradient :math:`\underline{F}`
with an assumed modified counterpart :math:`\bar{\underline{F}}` in the
computation of stresses. This modification is based on a multiplicative split
into volumetric and deviatoric parts:

.. math::

   \underline{F} = J^{1/3}\, \bar{\underline{F}}

where :math:`J = \det{\underline{F}}` is the Jacobian of the deformation
gradient. This formulation effectively avoids locking issues in nearly
incompressible materials while maintaining accuracy.

The :math:`\bar{F}` formulation is particularly suited for low-order finite
elements and is applicable to arbitrary material models. It ensures quadratic
rates of convergence in Newton-Raphson schemes.

This formulation is enabled by passing an additional parameter to the
:cxx:`Mechanics` behaviour integrator, as follows:

.. code:: c++

  const auto fbar_parameters = mfem_mgis::dict{
      {"Regularization", mfem_mgis::dict{{"FBar", mfem_mgis::dict{}}}}};
  mechanics.addBehaviourIntegrator(ctx, "Mechanics", "beam", library,
                                   behaviour, fbar_parameters) | or_die;

The FBar formulation is available in 3D and in plane strain (it is refused
in plane stress), for :code:`MGIS` built with :code:`TFEL`.

Faltus 2026 regularization
^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^

The regularization proposed by Faltus et al. in the context of contact
mechanics using a third medium is implemented here :cite:`faltus_deformation_2026`. This
regularization only applies to finite strain behaviours. Currently, this
regularization is only available for isotropic behaviours.

This regularization adds a contribution to the previous variational
operator in finite strain and can be derived from an energy :math:`W`
which penalizes the difference between the deformation gradient
:math:`\underline{F}` at a given quadrature point and its value
:math:`\bar{\underline{F}}` at the centroid of the element:

.. math::

    W\left(\underline{F}, \bar{\underline{F}}\right) =
    \alpha\,\left(\underline{F}-\bar{\underline{F}}\right)\,\colon\,
    \left(\underline{F}-\bar{\underline{F}}\right)

where :math:`\alpha` is a penalization coefficient.

This regularization is enabled by passing an additional parameter to the
:cxx:`Mechanics` behaviour integrator, as follows:

.. code:: c++

  const auto faltus_parameters = mfem_mgis::Parameters{
      {"Regularization",
       mfem_mgis::Parameters{
           {"Faltus2026",
            mfem_mgis::Parameters{{"PenalizationCoefficient", 1e11}}}}}};
  mechanics.addBehaviourIntegrator(ctx, "Mechanics", "ThirdMedium", library,
                                   behaviour2, faltus_parameters) | or_die;


Heat transfer behaviour integrators
-----------------------------------

Nonlinear heat transfer behaviour integrators compute the residual and jacobian
associated with the following variational operator:

.. math::
   
   -\int_{\Omega_r} \vec{j}\,\cdot\,\vec{\nabla} T^{\star}\,\mathrm{d}\,V

where:

- :math:`\Omega_r` is the domain occupied by the material of interest in the
  reference configuration,
- :math:`\vec{j}` is the heat flux,
- :math:`T^{\star}` is the virtual temperature.

These behaviour integrators are declared with the category
:code:`StationaryNonLinearHeatTransfer`. They require behaviours of the
general type (:code:`Behaviour::GENERALBEHAVIOUR`), and support:

- isotropic and orthotropic behaviours,
- plane strain, plane stress and tridimensional modeling hypotheses.

Transient heat transfer behaviour integrator
^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^

The behaviour integrator declared with the category
:code:`TransientHeatTransfer` computes the transient term of the heat
equation:

.. math::

   \int_{\Omega_r} \frac{\partial h}{\partial T}\,\dot{T}\,T^{\star}\,\mathrm{d}\,V

where :math:`h` is the enthalpy per unit of volume. It is declared as a
second behaviour integrator of a material, in addition to the stationary
one. Its inner forces
being the integral of :math:`N\,s`, :math:`s` being the thermodynamic force
of the behaviour, the behaviour must return the opposite of the rate of the
enthalpy, :math:`s=-\frac{\partial h}{\partial T}\,\dot{T}`, and its
tangent operator the opposite of
:math:`\frac{1}{\Delta\,t}\frac{\partial h}{\partial T}`. The gradient
of the behaviour is the temperature at the integration point: its value at
the beginning of the first time step must be initialised with the initial
temperature. This behaviour integrator is available for all modelling
hypotheses, requires a behaviour of the general type and accepts no
parameter.

Micromorphic damage behaviour integrators
-----------------------------------------

Micromorphic damage behaviour integrators compute the residual and jacobian
associated with the following variational operator:

.. math::
   
   \int_{\Omega_r} \vec{b}_{\chi}\,\cdot\,\vec{\nabla} d_{\chi}^{\star}+a_{\chi}\,d_{\chi}^{\star}\,\mathrm{d}\,V

where:

- :math:`\Omega_r` is the domain occupied by the material of interest in the
  reference configuration,
- :math:`b_{\chi}` is the thermodynamic force associated with the
  gradient of the micromorphic damage :math:`\vec{\nabla} d_{\chi}`,
- :math:`a_{\chi}` is the thermodynamic force associated with the
  micromorphic damage :math:`d_{\chi}`,
- :math:`d_{\chi}^{\star}` is the virtual micromorphic damage.

The micromorphic damage integrators available in :code:`MFEM/MGIS` support:

- isotropic behaviour in :math:`2D` (plane strain and plane stress) and :math:`3D` computations,
- orthotropic behaviours in :math:`2D`.
